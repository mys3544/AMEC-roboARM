# Speech recognition for Slovak, Czech and English on the M3's Orin NX, and boosting the command words (state of 27 October 2026)

Scope: which recogniser should run on the M3 Pro's Jetson Orin NX 16 GB (JetPack 6.2, shared with YOLO/YOLOE, Depth Anything and the ACT policy in TensorRT) for short spoken commands in Slovak, Czech and English, and how to push the recogniser towards our ~60 command words. Our baseline is the laptop console: Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3, int8 ONNX, sherpa-onnx on 4 CPU threads, greedy search, 154-300 ms for 1.4-3.5 s of audio, 277-450 ms from release to command ([console/README.md](../../console/README.md)); on 2026-10-21 it wrote spoken Slovak as Russian in Cyrillic or as Polish-looking text, patched by `llm.SOUNDS_LIKE` and prompt rules (79/79 on synthetic voices only; [NEXT.md](../../NEXT.md)). Runtimes and LLM formats are covered in `research_notes/Model export formats for our hardware/language_speech.md` and not repeated; this note adds the Slovak fine-tunes, the per-language tables, the int8 finding, the first Orin measurements and the boosting tools. Desk research only; nothing was run.

Labels: **[MEASURED-ORIN]** measured on a Jetson Orin (which one is said); **[MEASURED-other]** measured on other hardware or by the model's authors; **[CLAIM]** a vendor or author statement without a measurement in the source; **[2nd]** a secondary source or search summary not checked against the primary.

## 1. Published Slovak and Czech accuracy of the multilingual models

### Takeaway
Among open multilingual models, Canary-1B-v2 is clearly the most accurate on Slovak and Czech read speech (FLEURS 5.74 % and 7.86 % WER), Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 and Whisper large-v3 are level and ~50 % worse (8.8 / 11.0 and 8.4 / 11.3), Whisper large-v3-turbo is worse again on Slovak (10.7), and the 2026 streaming and LLM-style recognisers (Nemotron-3.5-ASR streaming, Qwen3-ASR) are 2-3x worse or lack Slovak. Parakeet's specific weakness for us is not its average WER but that it detects the language itself and cannot be told "Slovak": that is where the Cyrillic and Polish outputs come from.

### Evidence
- FLEURS WER, normalised, from the Canary/Parakeet technical report's Appendix B, Table 11 [MEASURED-other] — [arXiv 2509.14128](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14128):

  | model | sk | cs | pl | en |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Canary-1B-v2 (978M) | **5.74** | **7.86** | 6.64 | 4.50 |
  | seamless-m4t-v2-large | 7.42 | 9.31 | 8.76 | 6.15 |
  | Whisper large-v3 | 8.40 | 11.33 | 4.74 | 4.25 |
  | Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 | 8.82 | 11.01 | 7.31 | 4.85 |
  | seamless-m4t-medium | 11.16 | 14.89 | 14.84 | 6.99 |

- Averages over 24 languages (FLEURS, CoVoST, MLS): Canary-1B-v2 ~8.1, Parakeet v3 9.7, Whisper large-v3 9.9; English Open ASR average Parakeet v3 6.32, Canary 7.15, Whisper large-v3 7.44; RTFx (hardware not stated) Parakeet 3,333, Canary 749, Whisper large-v3 146 [MEASURED-other] — [arXiv 2509.14128](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14128)
- Parakeet v3 "automatically detects the language of the audio and transcribes it without requiring additional prompting"; the card describes no way to force a language; 600M parameters, CC-BY-4.0, 25 languages including Russian, Ukrainian and Bulgarian (Cyrillic) and Polish — [nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3). Canary takes `source_lang` and `target_lang` (KInIT's usage example passes `source_lang="sk"`) — [kinit/canary-1b-v2-sk](https://huggingface.co/kinit/canary-1b-v2-sk)
- Whisper large-v3-turbo on FLEURS Slovak 10.7 % (base), large-v3 9.2 %, medium 18.7 %, small 36.1 %; on Common Voice 21 Slovak turbo 31.7 %, large-v3 20.8 % [MEASURED-other] — [SloPalSpeech, arXiv 2509.19270](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19270v1) (the 9.2 vs 8.40 difference for large-v3 is between two papers' normalisations)
- Nemotron-3.5-ASR streaming 0.6B (June 2026, OpenMDW-1.1, cache-aware RNN-T, 80 ms-1.12 s chunks; "NVIDIA Jetson" in its hardware list without naming Orin): FLEURS with LangID sk 25.08 % (80 ms) to 21.28 % (1.12 s), cs 24.18 % to 20.41 %, pl 19.88 % to 15.15 %, en 9.43 % to 7.91 %; in "auto" mode 1-3 points worse [MEASURED-other] — [nvidia/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b)
- Qwen3-ASR-0.6B (Apache-2.0, Jan 2026): 30 languages with Czech and Polish but **not Slovak**; Czech only inside an aggregate "Fleurs††" group at 21.80 % WER; no hotword or biasing input, only `language` [CLAIM] — [Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-0.6B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-0.6B)
- Noise: on LibriSpeech-clean plus MUSAN noise, Parakeet v3 1.92 % (clean) -> 2.62 % (5 dB) -> 4.82 % (0 dB) -> 12.21 % (-5 dB); Canary-1B-v2 2.18 -> 2.80 -> 5.08 -> 19.38 % [MEASURED-other] — [arXiv 2509.14128](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14128). The Parakeet card's average over benchmarks: clean 6.34 %, 10 dB 7.12, 5 dB 8.23, 0 dB 11.66, -5 dB 19.88 [MEASURED-other] — [model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)

### Inferences
- The 2026-10-21 failures fit language misidentification more than poor Slovak acoustics: the words came out in Cyrillic and Polish spellings, i.e. Parakeet picked a neighbouring language. A model that is told the language (Canary, Whisper) or that only knows Slovak (a fine-tune, section 2) removes that failure mode outright.
- FLEURS is read Wikipedia sentences by adults at close range; our sentences are 2-4 s commands spoken across a robot with motors running. The published numbers rank the models; only our own test set (report, evaluation plan) can say what a command costs.

### Gaps
- No published WER on short spoken commands in Slovak or Czech for any model (looked: the model cards above, KInIT's cards, the SloPalSpeech paper, the Open ASR leaderboard paper's multilingual track, which has no cs/sk).
- No language-ID accuracy figure for Parakeet v3 on Slovak (the technical report does not discuss it).

## 2. Slovak fine-tunes (KInIT, NaiveNeuron) and the price: they forget the other languages

### Takeaway
Slovak groups have released fine-tunes of every relevant model in 2025-2026. KInIT's Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 fine-tune is the one to try first: 7.92 % WER on Common Voice 24 (base 9.07) and 6.62 % on KInIT's noise-augmented internal set (base 24.32 %), equal to their Canary fine-tune (6.37 %) at a fraction of the cost and drop-in for our sherpa-onnx code after an ONNX export. Every card warns of catastrophic forgetting: the fine-tunes lose the other 24 languages, so a Slovak-tuned model cannot also be our English recogniser, and nothing is published on how it treats Czech. For Czech no comparable fine-tune was found; base Canary-1B-v2 is the best published Czech model.

### Evidence
- **kinit/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3-sk** (CC-BY-4.0, NeMo checkpoint, full fine-tune, 3 epochs, AdamW, bf16, Devana HPC; data: SloPalSpeech, Common Voice 24, TEDxSK/JumpSK, FLEURS, municipal councils, read literature, internal recordings; 75 % of samples noise-augmented with phone noise, background speech, noise, white noise, packet loss): CV24 test (5,239 utterances) 7.92 % WER / 2.06 % CER vs base 9.07 / 2.54; internal set (9,317 utterances, two thirds noise-augmented, not public) 6.62 % vs base 24.32 %; "performance on the base model's other 24 languages degrades significantly"; trained mostly on short utterances; timestamps not evaluated after fine-tuning; "Parakeet is the faster of the two" [MEASURED-other] — [model card](https://huggingface.co/kinit/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3-sk)
- **kinit/canary-1b-v2-sk** (CC-BY-4.0): CV24 8.27 % vs base 14.00 %; internal 6.37 % vs base 14.39 %; speech translation lost; use with `source_lang="sk"` [MEASURED-other] — [model card](https://huggingface.co/kinit/canary-1b-v2-sk)
- **KInIT Whisper fine-tunes** (Apache-2.0 / MIT, v2 with noise augmentation): large-v3-sk CV24 7.68 % (base 19.70), internal 5.67 % (base 16.22); large-v3-turbo-sk (809M) CV24 9.29 % (base 29.23), internal 5.60 % (base 18.35); internal-set WER for medium-sk 6.28 % (base 29.19), small-sk 8.64 % (47.90), base-sk 14.01 % (76.38), tiny-sk 20.04 % (99.47); "fine-tuning exclusively on Slovak data significantly degrades performance on other languages" [MEASURED-other] — [whisper-large-v3-sk](https://huggingface.co/kinit/whisper-large-v3-sk), [whisper-large-v3-turbo-sk](https://huggingface.co/kinit/whisper-large-v3-turbo-sk); the collection lists canary-sk, parakeet-sk and six Whisper sizes (tiny 37.8M, base 72.6M, small 0.2B, medium 0.8B, turbo 0.8B, large 2B) — [KInIT ASR collection](https://huggingface.co/collections/kinit/automatic-speech-recognition-6a42684efb87315cc9da3247)
- **NaiveNeuron** (Comenius University / VÚB): turbo-sk FLEURS 10.7 -> 6.4 %, CV21 31.7 -> 13.2 %; large-v3-sk FLEURS 9.2 -> 5.5 %; trained on SloPalSpeech, 2,806 h of parliament; MIT; "multilingual performance is not guaranteed"; occasional hallucinations [MEASURED-other] — [whisper-large-v3-turbo-sk](https://huggingface.co/NaiveNeuron/whisper-large-v3-turbo-sk); [arXiv 2509.19270](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19270v1)
- **Czech:** the only Whisper large-v3 Czech fine-tune found reports 7.89 % WER on an unnamed evaluation set (probably Common Voice 13, ~2023) [2nd] — [mikr/whisper-large-v3-czech-cv13](https://huggingface.co/mikr/whisper-large-v3-czech-cv13); BUT-FIT's Czech model is air-traffic-control only — [BUT-FIT/whisper-ATC-czech-short](https://huggingface.co/BUT-FIT/whisper-ATC-czech-short). No Czech fine-tune of Parakeet v3 or Canary-1B-v2 was found among the Hub's listed fine-tunes of Parakeet v3 — [fine-tunes of parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3](https://huggingface.co/models?other=base_model%3Afinetune%3Anvidia%2Fparakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3)

### Inferences
- The internal-set gap (base Parakeet 24.32 % vs fine-tuned 6.62 %) is the noise-augmented part talking: KInIT trained with the kinds of noise our robot makes. That is the strongest argument for the fine-tune on the M3, stronger than the CV24 gain (9.07 -> 7.92).
- Language routing is unavoidable with fine-tunes. Three ways, cheapest first: (a) the language is a setting (the console's event page already has an SK / EN switch); (b) the wake phrase picks it ("Ahoj robot" -> Slovak/Czech model, "Hey robot" -> base model); (c) both recognisers run on every utterance and the one whose text the grammar parses wins, the session's language breaking ties (two Parakeets cost ~2x the time and memory of one). Czech should first be tried through the Slovak fine-tune: our command stems are shared (`kock`/`kostk`, `zober`/`vezmi`, `uprac`/`uklid`), so a Slovak-flavoured transcript of Czech speech may still parse. Untested.
- Canary-1B-v2 base with a forced `source_lang` is the one-model answer for all three languages and the best on Czech, but it is ~4.5x the CPU cost of Parakeet (onnx-asr RTFx 8 vs 36 on a 9800X3D; [onnx-asr](https://github.com/istupakov/onnx-asr)) and its decoder is autoregressive. Keep it as the Czech fallback and as the accuracy reference.

### Gaps
- KInIT's cards give no training hours, no release dates, no FLEURS evaluation and no Czech or English numbers after fine-tuning.
- No published ONNX or sherpa-onnx export of the KInIT Parakeet fine-tune was confirmed (the card mentions "a quantized variant elsewhere on the Hub" without a link); NeMo's export path for Parakeet v3 should apply unchanged, but that is our inference.

## 3. Running it on the Orin: the first Orin numbers, CPU vs GPU, and an int8 trap

### Takeaway
The first published Parakeet v3 measurement on a Jetson (an 8 GB Orin Nano, 25 W, JetPack 7.2.1, NeMo FP32) is fast enough: real-time factor 0.045, 0.33 s median per clip, but 6.35 GiB of memory because of the PyTorch stack. sherpa-onnx or onnx-asr avoid that overhead. On ARM CPU cores the int8 model runs at RTF ~0.09 with 4 threads, so a 2.5 s command costs roughly 0.25-0.35 s on the Orin's CPU without touching the GPU. But the int8 encoder we use on the laptop was shown this month to lose whole sentences on longer audio with pauses (17.2 % WER vs 5.9 % for fp32 on the same clips); on single utterances int8 scored 2.21 % (no fp32 figure beside it), so short commands may suffer little, yet it must be measured on our Slovak test set before we keep int8. Whisper turbo is 5x slower than Parakeet on the same Orin.

### Evidence
- **Orin Nano 8 GB (Turing Pi node), 25 W, JetPack 7.2.1, CUDA 13.2, 125 English clips** (article 26 Oct 2026) [MEASURED-ORIN]: Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 NeMo 3.0.0 / PyTorch FP32 RTF 0.0446, call latency P50 0.328 s / P95 0.519 s, process RSS 6.35 GiB (system 7.03 GiB, 347 MiB left), English WER 1.63 %, German 7.60 %; Parakeet CTC 0.6B RTF 0.036; faster-whisper 1.2.1 FP16: tiny.en 0.036 (P50 0.27 s), small 0.089 (0.68 s), large-v3-turbo 0.217 (1.74 s, RSS 3.72 GiB), turbo INT8-FP16 0.246 (1.96 s, 2.61 GiB) — [Turing Pi benchmark](https://turingpi.com/whisper-vs-parakeet-jetson-orin-nano-benchmarks/)
- **ARM CPU, sherpa-onnx, Parakeet TDT 0.6B v2 int8** (same encoder size as v3), RK3588 Cortex-A76, 7.4 s clip: RTF 0.220 (1 thread), 0.142 (2), 0.118 (3), 0.088 (4); v3 int8 files: encoder 622 MB, decoder 12 MB, joiner 6.1 MB [MEASURED-other] — [sherpa-onnx NeMo transducer models](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/pretrained_models/offline-transducer/nemo-transducer-models.html)
- **The int8 trap.** sherpa-onnx PR #4084 (merged 27 Oct 2026), LibriSpeech-based reproducer, CPU 4 threads: on concatenated 24-30 s clips with pauses, parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3-int8 greedy 17.19 % WER (6/20 clips missing >20 % of words); the fp32 v3 export (onnx-asr, greedy) 5.9 %; int8 encoder with fp32 decoder/joiner 21.2 % — "the int8 encoder is likely the cause"; on single utterances v3-int8 greedy 2.21 % [MEASURED-other] — [PR #4084](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/pull/4084). A separate greedy-search empty-output case was cured with `--dither=0.00003` [MEASURED-other] — [issue #3267](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/issues/3267)
- **GPU paths:** onnx-asr RTFx for Parakeet v2/v3: 9800X3D CPU 36, Cortex-A53 1.0, T4 CUDA 57, RTX 5070 Ti TensorRT 320; Canary v2: 8 / n/a / 21 / 36 [MEASURED-other] — [onnx-asr](https://github.com/istupakov/onnx-asr). On Jetson, NeMo needs the Jetson-specific torch wheels (jp6/cu126 index) — [HF discussion](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3/discussions/21); a user hit CUDA out-of-memory running canary-1b-v2 under NeMo on a 64 GB AGX Orin while canary-1b-flash ran [2nd] — [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/oom-error-running-canary-1b-v2-on-jetson-agx-orin-memory-not-actually-full/349754)
- **NVIDIA's own Jetson speech stacks do not cover our case:** Riva's current embedded support lists Jetson Thor / JetPack 7 [2nd] — [Riva support matrix](https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/riva/user-guide/docs/support-matrix/support-matrix.html); TensorRT Edge-LLM supports Qwen3-ASR and Nemotron-3.5-ASR, not Parakeet, and wants JetPack 7.2 on Orin — [Edge-LLM supported models](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-Edge-LLM/0.11.0/user_guide/getting_started/supported-models.html)
- **GGUF route:** canary-1b-v2 in GGUF for transcribe.cpp: Q8_0 1.14 GB, Q4_K_M 735 MB, LibriSpeech-clean WER unchanged (1.91-1.94 %); CUDA support and language forcing not documented [CLAIM] — [handy-computer/canary-1b-v2-gguf](https://huggingface.co/handy-computer/canary-1b-v2-gguf)
- **Laptop baseline** (i7-10875H, our measurement): 4 threads 207 ms for 2.5 s of audio — [console/README.md](../../console/README.md)

### Inferences
- Estimate for the Orin NX: Parakeet int8 on 4 A78AE cores ~0.25-0.35 s for a 2.5 s command (RK3588 A76 ratio); fp16 on the GPU through onnxruntime's CUDA provider ~0.1 s (Orin Nano NeMo FP32 ratio, the NX's GPU being the same Ampere generation at higher clocks). Both meet the budget; the choice is about contention (CPU: ROS 2 and the bridge; GPU: the ACT's 10 Hz loop and the detectors) and about int8 accuracy.
- Memory: an fp16 Parakeet in ONNX Runtime should be ~1.2-1.5 GB resident, int8 ~0.7-1 GB; two of them (Slovak fine-tune + base) fit, but the M3's free memory with its perception stack running has to be read with `tegrastats` first.
- Avoid NeMo/PyTorch on the robot for inference: 6.35 GiB RSS on the Orin Nano for a model whose int8 ONNX is 640 MB.
- JetPack 6.2 is CUDA 12.6 while onnxruntime deprecates its CUDA 12 packages (v1.27); pin the onnxruntime-gpu build from the Jetson index rather than upgrading ([language_speech.md](../Model%20export%20formats%20for%20our%20hardware/language_speech.md) section 5).

### Gaps
- No Orin NX measurement of any multilingual recogniser was found (looked: Turing Pi, Jetson AI Lab, NVIDIA forums, the Parakeet and Canary model discussions, sherpa-onnx docs). The Orin Nano number is English, NeMo FP32, and on JetPack 7.2.1, not our 6.2.
- No published comparison of int8 vs fp16 Parakeet on Slovak.

## 4. Boosting the command words: NeMo GPU-PB, sherpa-onnx hotwords, Whisper prompts and grammars

### Takeaway
All three toolchains now offer decode-time boosting without retraining, but each is young for our model: NeMo's GPU phrase boosting (GPU-PB / TurboBias) supports TDT and Canary and raised key-phrase F-score by 8-10 points greedy and 17-23 points with beam search at a 2-5 % speed cost, measured only in English on 108M-parameter models; a user reported it "did not really work" for Parakeet TDT. sherpa-onnx hotwords need `modified_beam_search`, which for NeMo TDT models hallucinated or returned empty text on a fifth to a third of requests until a fix merged on 27 October 2026 (not yet in a release). Whisper offers `hotwords` / `initial_prompt` in faster-whisper and a GBNF grammar in whisper.cpp. Our regex post-correction (`SOUNDS_LIKE`) stays useful as the last, deterministic layer. Boosting cannot cure the wrong-language problem; a language-forced or Slovak-only recogniser does.

### Evidence
- **NeMo GPU-PB:** "supported for CTC, RNN-T/TDT, and AED (Canary) models", shallow fusion at decoding, no retraining; phrases via `boosting_tree.key_phrases_file` (one per line); `context_score` 1.0, `depth_scaling` 2.0 for CTC/RNN-T/TDT and 1.0 for Canary, `boosting_tree_alpha` tuned; TDT with `greedy_batch` or `malsd_batch`, Canary with `beam` (greedy at beam 1); "capitalize key phrases in advance" for models that capitalise; no per-phrase weights. CTC-WS needs a CTC head (hybrid models only); Flashlight word boosting is CTC-with-lexicon only and the only one with per-word weights — [NeMo word boosting docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo-framework/user-guide/latest/nemotoolkit/asr/asr_customization/word_boosting.html)
- **TurboBias** (ASRU 2025): on 108M FastConformer models, English only, 200-991 phrases: CTC greedy F-score 35.0 -> 64.8 (CSTalks), RNN-T greedy 42.5 -> 70.4, RNN-T beam 44.2 -> 82.9, AED greedy 52.6 -> 75.6; WER 12.8 -> 10.7 (RNN-T greedy); RTFx loss 2-5 %; precision falls (97 -> 90 %) as recall rises; 20K phrases cost ~5 % speed; greedy boosting "can distort words outside the list" [MEASURED-other] — [arXiv 2508.07014](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07014)
- A streaming comparison found CTC-WS better than GPU-PB under greedy decoding (one hypothesis cannot recover a missed phrase) [2nd] — [arXiv 2605.18222](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.18222); a NeMo issue reported that phrase boosting worked with Canary (AED) but "did not really work" for Parakeet v2 [2nd] — [NeMo issue #14500](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/NeMo/issues/14500)
- **sherpa-onnx hotwords:** "Only transducer models support hotwords", with `modified_beam_search`; per-phrase score after `:`; default `hotwords_score` 1.5; side effects seen (words merged, near words changed) — [sherpa-onnx hotwords docs](https://k2-fsa.github.io/sherpa/onnx/hotwords/index.html). NeMo transducer beam search + hotwords added in PR #3077 — [PR #3077](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/pull/3077); with Parakeet v3 int8 it returned "Yeah." or empty text on ~10 of 30 requests even with an empty hotwords file (March 2026) [MEASURED-other] — [issue #3267](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/issues/3267); fixed by per-frame beam buckets, NeMo-style duration expansion, boost used only for candidate selection and a new `length_reward` (0.5 recommended for v3: concatenated-clip WER 40.89 % on master -> 2.91 %), merged 27 Oct 2026 [MEASURED-other] — [PR #4084](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/pull/4084); v2.0.0 (23 Oct) added hotwords to modified beam search for **streaming** NeMo transducers — [sherpa-onnx releases](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/releases)
- **Whisper:** faster-whisper's `transcribe()` takes `hotwords` ("Hotwords/hint phrases to provide the model with. Has no effect if prefix is not None.") and `initial_prompt` — [faster_whisper/transcribe.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper/master/faster_whisper/transcribe.py); whisper.cpp's `command` example takes `--grammar` (GBNF) and `--grammar-penalty` to scale down non-grammar tokens [2nd, mirrors] — [whisper.cpp command.cpp mirror](https://huggingface.co/datasets/echodict/whisper.cpp/blob/main/examples/command/command.cpp), [openHAB Whisper STT docs](https://openhab.org/addons/voice/whisperstt)
- **Ours:** `SOUNDS_LIKE` regexes put back "valec", "zober", "uprac", "stôl" (including from Cyrillic) before the parser reads the text; told in the prompt instead, the 4B began picking cubes for them (64/71) — [console/llm.py](../../console/llm.py), [console/README.md](../../console/README.md)

### Inferences
- Order of trial: (1) a recogniser that knows the language (Slovak fine-tune, or Canary with `source_lang`) — removes the script errors; (2) then boosting of the command list (~60 words in three languages, with their inflected forms: `kocku`, `kocky`, `kostku`), measured by command exact-match, not WER; (3) keep `SOUNDS_LIKE` for the residue. For Parakeet in sherpa-onnx, boosting means building sherpa-onnx from master (PR #4084) and `length_reward` 0.5; for Canary in NeMo, GPU-PB with `depth_scaling 1.0`.
- Boosting raises false accepts of the boosted words (TurboBias precision 97 -> 90 %). For us a boosted "zober" heard in idle chatter is harmless (the validator and the wake word gate it), but a boosted robot name or "všetky" could change the target; never boost "stop" in the command recogniser to emulate a stop lane.
- A grammar-constrained Whisper (whisper.cpp GBNF over the command language) is the strongest form of "boosting" and turns the recogniser into a parser; worth one experiment with KInIT whisper-small-sk or medium-sk, whose speed on the Orin (RTF ~0.09 for small on the Orin Nano) is acceptable.

### Gaps
- No boosting result for any multilingual or non-English model was found (TurboBias is English-only; NeMo docs give no numbers; sherpa-onnx examples are English and Chinese).
- Whether GPU-PB now works for Parakeet TDT v3 greedy decoding (issue #14500 predates the docs' TDT entry) is unverified.
- faster-whisper's `hotwords` effect size on short commands: not found (searched the README and code only).

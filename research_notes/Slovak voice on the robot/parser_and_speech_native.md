# From words to one command on the Orin: grammar, small LLM parser, and speech-native models (state of 27 October 2026)

Scope: the step after the recogniser on the M3's Orin NX 16 GB: turning a Slovak, Czech or English transcript into one validated command (pick by colour or size, pick_named, fetch, clean_up, sort, look, find, home, stop, unknown; robot m3, x3 or both), and whether a speech-native model can skip the transcript and go from audio to the command. Our baseline runs on the laptop: `voice.parse` (a hand-written grammar, stop first) and `llm.Brain` (Qwen3.5 4B Q4_K_M in llama.cpp b11617 on the RTX 2070 Super, JSON held to a schema, 79/79 on `llm_cases.json`, ~0.65 s median; Qwen3.5 2B 42/45 at 364 ms; Gemma 4 E2B 37/45, E4B 43/45) — [console/llm.py](../../console/llm.py), [console/README.md](../../console/README.md). Runtimes, quantisation and the Qwen3.5 prefix-cache issue are in `research_notes/Model export formats for our hardware/language_speech.md` sections 1-3 and only referenced here. Desk research only.

Labels: **[MEASURED-ORIN]**, **[MEASURED-other]**, **[CLAIM]**, **[2nd]** as in `asr_slovak_czech_on_orin.md`.

## 1. The laptop's 4B parser is too slow on the Orin as it stands

### Takeaway
On the Orin NX a small LLM's time is decode time, and our schema makes the model write every field: ~40-50 output tokens per command. At the best published Orin NX speed for Qwen3.5 4B (27 tokens/s in TensorRT Edge-LLM, INT4, on JetPack 7.2) that is ~1.7 s, and llama.cpp on our JetPack 6.2 is slower; at 0.8B (96 tokens/s) it is ~0.5 s. Shortening the output to ~10-15 tokens and caching the ~2,000-token prompt prefix matter as much as the model size: an uncached prefix alone costs 0.8 s (0.8B) to 2.6 s (4B).

### Evidence
- **Orin NX 16 GB, TensorRT Edge-LLM 0.10.0, JetPack 7.2 / CUDA 13.2 / TRT 10.16, batch 1, INT4 AWQ** [MEASURED-ORIN, NVIDIA]: decode Qwen3.5-0.8B 95.8 t/s, 2B 47.9 t/s, 4B 27.0 t/s, Qwen3-0.6B 124.9, Qwen3-1.7B 59.0, Qwen3-4B-2507 32.2; prefill of ~62 tokens 35.5 / 56.2 / 120.1 ms; 2,048-token prefill 841 / 1,161 / 2,558 ms (0.8B / 2B / 4B) — [Edge-LLM benchmarks](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-Edge-LLM/0.11.0/user_guide/performance/performance-benchmarks.html); Edge-LLM is officially supported on Orin with JetPack 7.2 and "compatible/experimental" on 6.2 — [Seeed wiki](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/deploy_tensorrt_edge_llm_on_jetpack7.2/) (via language_speech.md section 2)
- **llama.cpp on Orins** [MEASURED-ORIN]: Orin NX, Gemma 4 E4B Q4_K_M ~13 t/s on mainline, ~18 t/s with MTP speculative decoding (June 2026) — [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/llama-cpp-mtp-lifted-llm-performance-of-jetson-orin/372493); Orin NX at MAXN SUPER, ~15.6 GiB usable to CUDA, Gemma 4 26B-A4B Q2_K_XL 19.5 t/s — [dnhkng blog](https://dnhkng.github.io/posts/jetson-orin-nx-vram-tuning/); Orin Nano Super, Cosmos-Reason2-2B: llama.cpp Q4_K_M 38 t/s vs Edge-LLM W4A16 60 t/s, text TTFT 33 vs 29 ms — [jetson-orin-nano-benchmarks](https://github.com/hokwangchoi/jetson-orin-nano-benchmarks); Orin Nano 8 GB at 25 W, Qwen3 1.7B: prompt 1,408 t/s, decode 39 t/s [2nd] — [Turing Pi](https://turingpi.com/llm-tokens-per-second-memory-bandwidth/)
- **Our prompt and output:** system prompt (~60 lines of rules and the taught names) + 9 few-shot pairs + the sentence; the schema requires all seven fields (`action, robot, kind, colour, size_mm, name, by`) with nulls, `max_tokens` 256, `temperature` 0, thinking off — [console/llm.py](../../console/llm.py)
- **llama.cpp and Qwen3.5's hybrid layers:** prefix reuse for Gated-DeltaNet models depends on context checkpoints with a default `--checkpoint-min-step` of 8192 tokens; open issues report "forcing full prompt re-processing" with Qwen3.5 — [language_speech.md](../Model%20export%20formats%20for%20our%20hardware/language_speech.md) section 1, [llama.cpp #24587](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/24587)
- Constrained decoding makes 0.6-4B models emit valid JSON but leaves a semantic gap; keep the validator — [arXiv 2609.23742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23742) (RESEARCH.md P6)

### Inferences
- Output-token arithmetic (our estimate, Qwen tokenizer): the full seven-field JSON is ~40-50 tokens; a compact form such as `{"a":"pick","r":"m3","k":"cube","c":"red","s":null}` with null fields omitted is ~15-20; a positional line `pick m3 cube red -` held by a GBNF grammar ~8-10. At Edge-LLM speeds: 4B full ~1.7 s, 2B full ~1.0 s, 0.8B full ~0.5 s, 0.8B positional ~0.1 s, all plus ~35-120 ms prefill of the new tokens when the prefix is cached.
- llama.cpp on JetPack 6.2 should land at ~60-65 % of Edge-LLM decode speed (the Orin Nano Super ratio 38/60); good enough for a 0.8B with a short output, not for the 4B.
- Measure whether grammar-forced tokens each cost a full forward pass in llama-server (likely, as no "jump-forward" path is documented for it); if they do, the compact schema is the cheapest speed-up we have.

### Gaps
- No llama.cpp measurement of Qwen3.5 0.8B/2B/4B on an Orin NX was found (looked: Jetson AI Lab, NVIDIA forums, Turing Pi, dnhkng, openzeka); no Edge-LLM numbers on JetPack 6.2.

## 2. Grammar first, a distilled 0.8B second, the 4B as teacher

### Takeaway
For a closed command set the fastest and most predictable parser is the grammar we already have: it decides stops at once and parses most simple commands in under a millisecond. The LLM earns its place on paraphrases, the taught names and the two-robot sentences (M3.1). On the Orin it should be a Qwen3.5 0.8B (or 2B) fine-tuned on paraphrases written by a large model and labelled by our 4B, emitting a compact command; the 4B stays on the laptop as the reference and teacher. Every route ends in the validator.

### Evidence
- Our bench: Qwen3.5 2B 42/45 untuned vs 4B 45/45; after prompt rules and `SOUNDS_LIKE`, 4B 79/79 — [console/README.md](../../console/README.md); the distillation plan (a large open model on the 5090 writes thousands of Slovak/Czech/English paraphrases with their JSON, a LoRA turns Qwen3.5-0.8B or -2B into the parser, measured against the large model by exact match and latency) — [PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md) M3
- Qwen3.5 small models (Mar 2026, Apache-2.0) are hybrid Gated-DeltaNet models with a 248k vocabulary and MTP heads; the tied LM head is a large share of a 0.8B model's bytes per token — [language_speech.md](../Model%20export%20formats%20for%20our%20hardware/language_speech.md) section 3
- Audio errors in voice tool-calling fall mostly on argument values, which favours exact-match scoring over intent accuracy — [arXiv 2605.15104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15104) (RESEARCH.md P6)

### Inferences
- Parse order on the robot: (1) stop words -> halt, done; (2) grammar -> if it yields one complete command with every needed slot, done; (3) otherwise the 0.8B/2B parser; (4) the validator (known objects, robot free, box seen, both robots free for M3.1) and, for multi-step plans, a read-back with a 2 s cancel window.
- Train the small parser on recogniser-shaped text, not clean text: run the paraphrases through Piper/Supertonic TTS and back through the chosen recogniser (with noise), so the parser learns the recogniser's errors instead of `SOUNDS_LIKE` growing by hand. This is also the cheapest way to keep `SOUNDS_LIKE` short.
- A no-LLM classifier (a small multilingual encoder for intent + slots) would be faster still, but it handles the taught names and the "both robots" sentences less naturally than a generative parser; not worth building unless the 0.8B fails.

## 3. Speech-native models: a comparison for P6, not the stack

### Takeaway
No open speech-native model in October 2026 has published Slovak or Czech speech accuracy and runs comfortably on the Orin. Gemma 4 E2B (Apache-2.0, a 305M-parameter audio encoder, 30 s clips) evaluates its ASR on 12 languages without Czech or Slovak, its llama.cpp audio path had a transcription bug and no server endpoint after it was added, and its text parsing was our weakest (37/45). Ultravox v0.5 1B (Whisper-turbo encoder + a frozen Llama-3.2-1B, MIT) inherits Whisper's languages but reports no ASR accuracy. Qwen3.5-Omni covers Czech but is API-only, Voxtral 3 lists Czech and Slovak in its FLEURS chart but is an API product with a separate LLM for tools. So the cascade (recogniser -> grammar/small LLM -> validator) is the deployable stack, and Gemma 4 E2B is the speech-native arm of P6's comparison, run first on the laptop.

### Evidence
- **Gemma 4 E2B / E4B:** 2.3B effective (5.1B with embeddings) / 4.5B effective; ~300M audio encoder; "Audio supports a maximum length of 30 seconds"; ASR and speech translation in "multiple languages"; FLEURS 0.09 / 0.08; Apache-2.0; native tool use family-wide — [Gemma 4 model card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_4). Technical report: USM-based 305M encoder, 40 ms chunks, 12 Conformer layers; FLEURS evaluated on en, ko, ja, de, fr, hi, es, it, pt-br, ru, ar, zh only (E2B average 0.090: en 0.080, de 0.076, ru 0.084); Czech, Slovak and Polish absent from all audio tables; transcription 17 % better than Gemma 3n; no spoken function-calling evaluation [MEASURED-other] — [arXiv 2607.02770](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02770v1)
- llama.cpp added Gemma 4's audio encoder (PR #21421, BF16 mmproj recommended); a later issue reported it "does not faithfully transcribe" (#21820, followed by a causal-attention fix #21824); `llama-server` had no `input_audio` path (#21868) [2nd] — [PR #21421](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/21421), [issue #21820](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/21820), [issue #21868](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/21868). TensorRT Edge-LLM lists gemma-4-E2B-it/E4B-it under "Text, image, and audio input" but does not say which are validated on Orin — [Edge-LLM supported models](https://nvidia.github.io/TensorRT-Edge-LLM/0.11.0/user_guide/getting_started/supported-models.html)
- Our text-only bench: Gemma 4 E2B 37/45 (541 ms), E4B 43/45 (1,067 ms) on the 2070 Super; on the Orin NX E4B decodes ~13 t/s in llama.cpp [MEASURED-ORIN, forum] — [console/README.md](../../console/README.md), [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/llama-cpp-mtp-lifted-llm-performance-of-jetson-orin/372493)
- **Ultravox v0.5 1B:** Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct frozen, whisper-large-v3-turbo encoder fine-tuned, adapter trained by distillation from the text model's logits; "42 languages" tag; no ASR WER reported; MIT — [fixie-ai/ultravox-v0_5-llama-3_2-1b](https://huggingface.co/fixie-ai/ultravox-v0_5-llama-3_2-1b); a ggml-org GGUF exists — [ggml-org/ultravox-v0_5-llama-3_2-1b-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/ultravox-v0_5-llama-3_2-1b-GGUF); the v0.6 release notes list 42 languages including Czech and Slovak [2nd] — [Ultravox releases](https://github.com/fixie-ai/ultravox/releases)
- **Qwen:** Qwen3-Omni (open, 30B-A3B) has 19 speech-input languages, Czech not among them; Qwen3.5-Omni (Mar 2026) recognises 113 languages including Czech but is offered through API and Qwen Chat only [2nd] — [Qwen3-Omni](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-Omni), [Qwen3.5-Omni report](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15804v2)
- **Voxtral 3** (Mistral, evaluation notes dated 2026-10-20): transcription in 50+ languages; its FLEURS charts include CS and SK (macro-averages only); Mini Transcribe 3 ~4B; realtime "sub-500 ms"; tool calling is done by a separate LLM, not from audio; open weights not stated [CLAIM] — [Mistral: Voxtral 3](https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-3/); Voxtral Transcribe 2 / Realtime (Feb 2026) covered 13 languages without Czech or Slovak [2nd] — [Mistral: Voxtral Transcribe 2](https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-transcribe-2/)
- Server-side speech tool-calling benchmarks are English-centric (MTVA-Bench, CORTIS, From Text to Voice) — RESEARCH.md P6

### Inferences
- P6's comparison stays meaningful and novel because of the language: cascaded (Parakeet-sk + grammar + 0.8B) vs Gemma 4 E2B audio -> tool call, scored by exact command match per language and p95 latency, on the same recordings. Run Gemma 4 E2B in Transformers on the laptop or hekate first (the reference implementation, avoiding llama.cpp's audio bugs), then on the Orin through Edge-LLM only if the lab moves a robot to JetPack 7.2 or through llama.cpp's CLI path.
- Expect the speech-native model to lose on Slovak argument values (colours, sizes, names) and win nothing on latency on the Orin: its 2.3B-effective decoder at ~13-25 t/s writes the same JSON tokens as a cascade's LLM, after a heavier audio encoder.

### Gaps
- No Slovak or Czech speech-input accuracy for Gemma 4, Gemma 3n, Ultravox, Phi-4-multimodal or Voxtral Mini models was found (looked: their model cards, the Gemma 4 technical report, Mistral's pages, Ultravox's cards and releases).
- Whether llama.cpp's Gemma 4 audio transcription is fixed in current builds (b119xx) is unverified.
